Router lights are useful when something is wrong and annoying when it is not. A router in a bedroom or a lounge can light the room at night. The app has a switch for this: **Turn the lights off** under [System](../router/system.md).

## What the switch does

| Setting | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Lights on | Normal. Power, internet, Wi-Fi and port activity show |
| Lights off | Status lights go dark. The router works exactly as before |

Turning the lights off changes nothing about speed, range or safety. It saves a little power and a lot of annoyance.

## Caution

With the lights off you cannot tell at a glance that the internet light has gone red or that the router restarted. Use the app instead: **Overview** shows connection state and uptime. If you are chasing a fault, turn the lights back on first.

> [!TIP]
> A router that restarts by itself shows a short uptime in Overview. If the uptime keeps resetting, the cause is probably power or heat, not the lights.

## Scheduling the lights

Many models have a separate schedule that dims or turns off the lights during set hours. This can hide a problem that happens overnight, such as a restart at 03:00. The same clock rules as for a [Wi-Fi schedule](../wireless/wifi-schedule.md) apply: the router clock must be right.

## Steps

1. Open **System** and find **Turn the lights off**.
2. Switch it. The preview shows the old and new value. This is a low-risk change, so it needs just a confirm.
3. Check the router. The lights dim or go out within a few seconds.
4. To reverse it, switch it back or use the [change history](../safety/change-history.md).
